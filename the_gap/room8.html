The Research Gap

What is it
A research gap is a specific absence in the existing literature — something that hasn't been studied, built, tested, or critically examined — that your project addresses. It is not a complaint that people haven't done your exact project. It is an argument: here is what exists; here is what it doesn't do; here is what that costs; here is what my work adds.
The gap statement structure

While [existing work] has established [what is known],

it has not addressed [the specific absence].

This matters because [the stakes — what the absence costs].

My project addresses this gap by [your approach and contribution].

The six gap types
Method — the technique hasn't been tried for this problem.
Application — the method hasn't been applied in this context.
Knowledge — the question simply hasn't been asked or answered.
Scale — findings haven't been tested at this scale or in this population.
Critical — an embedded assumption hasn't been examined or challenged.
Audience — the work hasn't reached, or been designed for, this group of people.
This week's tasks
1.Write a one-paragraph description of your project. Don't mention your gap yet — just describe what you're making and why.
2.Find three papers that relate to your project. For each: what's the question, the method, the contribution, the limitation?
3.Identify one thing each paper doesn't do that yours will. That's the beginning of your gap.
4.Write a gap statement using the structure above. It will be rough. That's fine. Bring it to the next session.
5.Ask yourself: what type of gap is mine? Method, application, knowledge, scale, critical, or audience? Be specific. If it's more than one, say which is primary.
